The model, in three steps

1

Fall World is the shop

One address. You open it, you see everything there is, you pick a thing and it runs. Nobody needs to know what a repo is — same as buying a game.

2

fall-os is what you install, and Didy lives in it

The operating system plus your own agent. It runs on your machine, on your electric. That is the whole product — everything else is gear it can pick up.

3

The rest are shelves

Run a trade. Sell. Make. Talk. Remember. Prove. You go to a shelf because you have a job to do, not because you know a tool's name.

The plan — four moves

Move 1One door

Fall World is the only address anybody is given. Every shelf, every build, reachable from it. No more sending people a repo link.

Done when: one URL answers "what have you got?"

Move 2One answer per job

Where seven apps do one job, pick the one that is furthest along and make the others modes inside it. The names stay as redirects so nothing anybody bookmarked breaks.

Done when: each shelf has a clear first choice instead of a list.

Move 3Hide the plumbing

The 814 companion repos are machine-minted bindings, not products. They stay where they are and come off every surface a person sees.

Done when: the shop shows 777 things, not 1591.

Move 4Say which ones are finished

This is the one that matters. Right now a rough prototype and a properly tested build look identical, so everything reads as equally solid — which means nothing does.

PrototypeIt exists and it opens. No promises.
WorksIt does the job end to end, and it is being used.
ProvenA machine tried to break it and failed, on hardware we do not own.

Done when: every build on every shelf shows one of these three, worked out from evidence rather than claimed.

Being straight about it: most of this is at Prototype. A small number are Proven — the ones with a mutation gate green on GitHub's own runners. Move 4 is what turns a big pile into a short honest list, and it is the only move that makes the other three worth doing.
