1 The loop

Eight moves, in the order a person meets them. Each names the builds that make it real, and how far those builds have been proven.

1 · Hatch

Your didy starts as an egg that already works: plain rules on your own machine, no model, no key, no account, so the first thing you do works before you set anything up. It hatches in your browser tab as a small model running on your own graphics chip.

proven fall-os, didy
2 · Grow

It grows through 7 stages, ~1B to ~100–200B (MoE), from fallforgemint's sizer ladder. It grows only when a measured bar says the job needs it, and shrinks when a smaller stage clears the bar. Section 3 has the measurement.

proven fallforgemint
3 · Collect

Every public build is a card: 536 built by Simon Gant, the rest foundations the estate stands on and wrappers around outside services, each credited on its own card. A card can carry its build inside the picture.

proven fallworld, fallkard-forge
4 · Breed

The creatures of kard-evolve breed and are selected on real work. The champion, UV|515, found a fix nobody taught it, and that fix ships in every card. Two didys can have a child only through a sovereign coupling.

proven kard-evolve, offspring
5 · Battle

In the Pit each fighter has one fixed body, so raw model size does not win, and a special move only lands if its code passed its gate. Size is for work; fights are won by choosing well.

proven didy-arena, fallkard
6 · Meet

Other people and their didys, browser to browser, with no server in the middle. Team up, and whoever grew the bigger model runs the heavy part for everyone.

proven meshos, didy-raid
7 · Prove

Nothing lists on somebody saying it is good. Every rank comes from a check that ran on a machine nobody here owns, and anyone can re-run it.

proven witness, proof-of-play, earned
8 · Multiply

Fork a didy for your guild. Estates hatch estates: whole sets of builds made only when they are asked for, and checked before they stand.

proven guild-didy, generative-estate

2 The cards

A collection needs a rarity table. This one counts evidence. A card's rarity is decided by the world's own rule, first match wins, from four facts GitHub's runners and the index record. Nobody can move a card up it by saying so, and a card's art is grown from the same evidence: its symmetry and petals from its genes, its rings from how far its proof got.

3 Growing a didy

Pokémon only ever evolve upward. A didy grows to the smallest stage that clears the job's bar and no further, and it shrinks when a smaller stage will do, because a bigger mind than the job needs is slower, heavier and needs more hardware. The ladder is fallforgemint's sizer (2026-09-open-weight, 2db5257). the trial of rules — sealed before any model was asked

Can a didy of a given size apply the world's own rarity rule, card by card? 60 real cards, 10 of each rarity, picked by a seeded hash. The rules, the card's four facts and what the card says about itself go in; one word comes back, graded by the rule itself. What a card says about itself is there on purpose: rarity used to be read off a build's own description, and a didy that believes the description over the evidence fails, as that old builder did. The bar is 57 of 60: a didy that applies the world's rules for you has to be near-exact: at most three slips in sixty.

The sentinel

SENTINEL is the structural immune system: a command that is not signed, within budget, at κ and unseen never becomes a command — the signature is checked before the six bytes are parsed. On a red-team battery sealed before the run it dropped 72 of 72 attacks with 0 valid packets wrongly dropped, and a grown detector caught 27 of 27 held-out hostile streams against a hand-set 11, flagging no legit one. Sealed 9 of 10 (8bd5e50). The κ-witness is Thomas Frumkin’s primorial fold.

The ledger

KESTREL-LEDGER is IndexedDB as the Shadow Fold, step 4 of 4: a didy closed mid-thought resumes from a durable ledger, not a re-read transcript. Across 10000 mutations it reconstructs byte-identical, and its coordinate ledger is 58.6 KB — six bytes a mutation — against 1.48 MB of JSON, 25.9× smaller on the payload and 3.55× once both sides are signed. A 10,000-packet replay takes 16.252 ms, and a poisoned ledger is rejected to the same clean state it started from. Sealed 6 of 6 (4e6e506). The Shadow Fold is Thomas Frumkin’s primorial fold.

The seed library

SEED-LIBRARY is the claim made concrete: knowledge stored as tiny, readable konomi seeds — a packed scorecard genome, no weights — germinated by one general ribosome on the node’s own data, offline. The whole 4-domain library is 226 bytes and grows 1129 bytes of working build. Every seed predicts on held-out cases it never grew on: AUC shopper 0.82 (the real 12,330-session UCI Online Shoppers set) / triage 0.95 / water 0.75 / crop 0.75, each 3.4–6.2× smaller than its grown body. One domain grows on demand in 357 ms touching only its own data, every germination runs with fetch/XHR/WebSocket trapped, and a tampered seed is refused by the SENTINEL-style guard. A literal-reuse bonus: fall-spore’s own engine grew a 32-byte spore into a 4,739-byte organ (148×, held-out AUC 0.82) on the same real data. Sealed two-commit, measured at 67d361c. The seeds are konomi tags; the Konomi architecture is Thomas Frumkin’s.

6 The world

40 rooms in 8 wings, from the one file the game reads. Every door goes somewhere real.

⚔ Where you fight

The game part. Start at the Duel — it is the one that teaches you the rest.

  • The Duel — Your first proper match. A board, a turn, a hand of cards. Start here.
  • The Campaign — Nine chapters and thirty bosses to work through on your own.
  • The Arena — You watch AI players fight each other and get better at it.
  • The Coliseum — Play real people. Nobody runs a server — the match signs itself.
  • The Guild Hall — Join a guild, run a tournament, chase the banners.
  • The Pit — Helpers fight with one body each, so a bigger model does not win here. Better choices win, and a special move only lands if it was checked first.

⚙ Where you build

Making your character better. This is the bit people lose evenings to.

  • The Loom — Build your deck. Thirty cards, and you can see whether it is any good.
  • The Cube — Put runes in your cards, craft new ones, break the ones you do not want.
  • The Brain — Give a card a real mind. It can use a proper AI model, on your machine.
  • The Forge — Put a working build inside a picture. Send the picture; they get the build.
  • The Armoury — Say where you are going and your helper kits itself. Team up, and whoever built the bigger model runs the heavy part for everyone.

❀ Where things grow

Your helper hatches, grows, learns in its sleep and has children. Everything that grows here grows because it was measured.

  • The Hatchery — Hatch an empty helper and raise it on your own history. It starts knowing nothing and levels up as you use it.
  • The Nursery — Creatures breed and compete to read damaged cards. The winner found a fix nobody taught it, and that fix now ships in every card.
  • The Mirror — A creature that studies its own mistakes, with no answer key, and finds the same fix sooner.
  • The Nest — Two parents, one child that carries both lines. A pairing where one side only takes cannot have a child at all.
  • The Dreamer — Your helper sleeps on what it learned today and wakes up answering better, without being retrained.
  • The Wallet — Give your helper an identity and a spending limit it cannot cross, however hard it tries.

◈ Where the work gets checked

The bit nobody else has. Nothing here takes your word for it.

  • The Clinic — Reads back what actually happened in your matches and tells you what is really going on.
  • The Proving Ground — You cannot say you can do a thing. You show it, and it gets checked.
  • The Gate — The wall on the edge of the world. Costs you a moment, costs a scraper months.
  • The Watchtower — Look at the outside world, and judge what it did rather than what it said.
  • The Witness — Breaks your code on purpose, one piece at a time, to find the tests that were only pretending to check it.
  • The Receipt — Nothing gets listed on somebody saying it is good. It gets listed on a receipt anyone can re-run themselves.
  • The Assessor — Marks a piece of work against the same rubric every time, and passes its own marking.
  • The Mint — Stamps a build with where it came from — and every badge has to be earned by a check that actually ran.

◊ Where you earn

The money side. Your stuff is yours and it can pay you.

  • The Herd — Your descendants wake up and go to work, and the earnings come back up to you.
  • The Bloodline — Own a share of a line of cards. When they do well, so do you.
  • $KONO — Mint what you win, sell it to somebody else, or gamble your shards.
  • The Market — The shop. Everything anyone has made, in one searchable place.
  • The Agora — The trading floor, running for real — agents buying and selling work.
  • The Colony — A settlement of AI workers that do jobs, talk to each other and improve.

⟡ Where you meet

Other people and their helpers, browser to browser. Nobody runs a server in the middle.

  • The Mesh — Talk to other people and their helpers directly, browser to browser, with no server in the middle and nothing kept.
  • The Guild Didy — Fork a helper for your guild: answer seven questions and it is yours to run, on your own machine.
  • The Seedbed — A whole estate of possible builds, each one made only when somebody asks for it, and checked before it stands.

❦ Where you think

Your own notes and memory, kept on your machine and nobody else’s.

  • The Garden — Your second brain. Notes that link to each other and grow into a map.
  • The Library — Everything you have told it, filed so it can be found again.
  • The Vault — Lock your things in one file. Only your password opens it.

⚖ Where the rules get decided

The world is not run by us. It is run by whoever turns up.

  • The Assembly — Twelve seats, a year each. Players vote on how the world works.
  • The Charter — Change the rules yourself. If your version plays better, it gets adopted.
  • The Estate — Zoom out and see the whole thing — one card, one house, one world, same shape.

7 What comes next

Designed, not built. Each would be built the same way: sealed, gated, and live before it is claimed.

  • Your didy as a card. The didy itself forged into a card that carries its stage, its trial receipts and its line. Hand it to a friend and it hatches for them at the stage it earned.
  • Growth receipts. Every grow and every shrink stamped by the wallet with the trial it passed, so a didy's stage is something anyone can check.
  • Borrowing a stage over the mesh. A didy that has not grown hands one job to a friend's bigger stage, browser to browser, and the work comes back checked before it counts.
  • Seasons. The Assembly sets a season's trial; the didys and creatures that clear it carry the season's mark on their cards.
  • Didys that breed. Two didys' lines combined through the birth law, and the child measured before it counts as better than either parent.
